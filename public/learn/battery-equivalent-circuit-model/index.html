Battery Equivalent-Circuit Model

Volta Lab does not simulate battery chemistry. It uses a small electrical circuit that behaves like a battery: a voltage source, a resistor and one resistor-capacitor pair.

Simulation only. This page explains a software model. It is educational material, not engineering or safety advice.

The circuit

≈ Engineering approximation This is a first-order Thevenin (1-RC) equivalent circuit. The terminal voltage while discharging at current I is:

Vt = OCV(SOC, T) − I · R0 − V1

  • OCV is the open-circuit voltage, which depends on state of charge and temperature.
  • R0 is the instantaneous series resistance: the immediate voltage sag when current starts.
  • V1 is the voltage across a resistor-capacitor pair R1, C1. It follows dV1/dt = (I · R1 − V1) / (R1 · C1), so the sag builds up and relaxes over seconds rather than instantly. Volta Lab advances it with an exact exponential step, which stays stable at any step size.

Counting charge

◆ Physical law State of charge is tracked by Coulomb counting: remove I · Δt of charge each step and divide by the capacity. If the remaining charge would go below zero, delivery is truncated, so an empty battery gives nothing and never creates energy.

From cell to pack

◆ Physical law Cells in series add voltage and cells in parallel add capacity: Vpack = Ns · Vcell, Ah = Np · Ahcell, Rpack = Ns · Rcell / Np.

◇ Illustrative assumption The default machine pack is 100 lithium-ion cells in series by 2 in parallel, 5 Ah per cell. That gives 10 Ah, about 372.6 V nominal and about 3.73 kWh nominal, starting at 90 % state of charge. This is an illustrative pack, not a commercial product.

Heat and protection

≈ Engineering approximation Battery heating uses a lumped thermal model (Cth · dT/dt equals heat generated minus heat lost to ambient). The model enforces a low-voltage cutoff, an empty-battery state, an over-discharge current limit and an over-temperature limit, each of which latches a protective shutdown.

Where the numbers come from

The shape of the open-circuit-voltage curve, the cutoff voltages and the coulombic efficiency are literature-typical values. The resistances, the time constant, their dependence on charge and temperature, and the thermal values are assumed by the author. No battery parameter in Volta Lab is a manufacturer datasheet value or a measurement.

What it does not do

There is no charging feature, no ageing or capacity fade, no thermal runaway, and no cell-to-cell imbalance. A 12 V lead-acid profile exists only inside the test suite to check the maths; the machine itself always uses the lithium-ion pack above.
