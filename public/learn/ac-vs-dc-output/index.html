AC vs DC Output

Direct current (DC) keeps one polarity and, in the ideal case, a constant value. Alternating current (AC) reverses direction many times per second. Volta Lab can produce either.

Simulation only. This page explains a software model. It is educational material, not engineering or safety advice.

DC

A DC source holds a steady voltage between its two terminals. A battery is naturally DC. In Volta Lab's DC mode the output is the DC-DC stage's output, with a displayed frequency of 0 Hz.

AC, peak and RMS

◆ Physical law A sinusoidal AC voltage is v(t) = √2 · Vrms · sin(2π f t). Its peak is Vpk = √2 · Vrms, and its period is T = 1 / f. The RMS value is the DC voltage that would heat a resistor equally, which is why AC voltages are normally quoted in RMS.

At the default 50 Hz, one cycle takes 20 ms.

Single-phase, two-wire

Volta Lab's AC output has two conductors, so it is single-phase and two-wire. The displayed voltage is the RMS voltage between Lead A and Lead B. It is not a three-phase line-to-line value, and the machine does not model any mains supply.

What the AC/DC selector does

≈ Engineering approximation The selector chooses which chain is active; it does not convert anything by itself.

  • DC: battery → DC-DC stage → output terminals.
  • AC: battery → DC-DC stage → DC link → H-bridge inverter → output terminals.

Switching mode re-solves the circuit and clears timers that belonged to the previous mode. It cannot create energy: only the solved currents change, and the energy ledger still has to balance. See Single-phase H-bridge inverter and DC-DC converter fundamentals.

Power

◆ Physical law For AC the real power is P = Vrms · Irms · cos φ, apparent power is S = Vrms · Irms, and the power factor is P / S. For a purely resistive load, φ = 0 and the power factor is 1. For DC, power is simply V · I.

What it does not do

There is no rectification stage in the machine itself. A bridge-rectifier model exists only inside the optional "AC-input supply" load type, which represents a device that rectifies the AC it receives.
