Five short lessons

Each lesson explains one block of the simulator and is based on how it is actually implemented. Every statement is tagged so you can tell what kind of claim it is:

  • ◆ Physical law: established physics that does not depend on this project.
  • ≈ Engineering approximation: a standard simplification, valid only within stated limits.
  • ◇ Illustrative assumption: a number or choice picked by the author to make the demonstration work, not a real product's value.
  • DC-DC Converter FundamentalsA DC-DC converter changes one steady voltage into another. Volta Lab needs one because its battery pack sits at a fixed voltage while the output can be set anywhere from 0 to 415 V.
  • Single-Phase H-Bridge InverterAn inverter turns direct current into alternating current. In AC mode, Volta Lab sends the DC-DC stage's output through an H-bridge to make a single-phase, two-wire AC output.
  • Battery Equivalent-Circuit ModelVolta Lab does not simulate battery chemistry. It uses a small electrical circuit that behaves like a battery: a voltage source, a resistor and one resistor-capacitor pair.
  • AC vs DC OutputDirect current (DC) keeps one polarity and, in the ideal case, a constant value. Alternating current (AC) reverses direction many times per second. Volta Lab can produce either.
  • Model Limitations and SafetyVolta Lab is a teaching tool. It runs entirely in your browser and cannot generate, measure or deliver real electricity. Please read this page before you trust any number it shows.

What Volta Lab is

Volta Lab is a browser-based 3D simulation of a battery, a DC-DC converter and a single-phase inverter feeding two output cables. You can switch between AC and DC, set the output between 0 and 415 V, bring the cable ends together to see a simulated fault, and read live telemetry. It does not simulate solar panels, grid connection or three-phase systems, and it never produces real electricity.
