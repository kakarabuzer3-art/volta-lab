Single-Phase H-Bridge Inverter

An inverter turns direct current into alternating current. In AC mode, Volta Lab sends the DC-DC stage's output through an H-bridge to make a single-phase, two-wire AC output.

Simulation only. This page explains a software model. It is educational material, not engineering or safety advice.

Four switches in an H

A full bridge has two switches on each side of the load. Closing one diagonal pair drives current through the load in one direction; closing the other pair reverses it. Switching between the pairs fast enough, and varying how long each is on, lets the average output follow a sine wave. This is sine-PWM (pulse-width modulation).

Modulation index and the DC link

≈ Engineering approximation For a single-phase full bridge in its linear range, the peak output voltage is the modulation index m times the DC-link voltage: Vac,pk = m · Vlink, and the RMS value is Vac,rms = Vac,pk / √2.

◇ Illustrative assumption The model limits m to 0.95. To deliver the 415 V RMS maximum, the peak is √2 × 415 ≈ 586.9 V, so the DC link must be at least about 586.9 / 0.95 ≈ 617.8 V. The model allows a link between 60 V and 700 V (assumed limits). If the link cannot reach what the requested AC voltage needs, the converter reports a modulation limit instead of pretending.

Real power comes from the DC side

◆ Physical law Only real power (the part that does work, P = Vrms · Irms · cos φ) must be supplied by the DC link. Reactive power circulates between the load and the bridge without being drawn from the battery. Volta Lab books link power as:

Plink = Pout,real + Pfixed + ksw · Sout + Rbridge · Irms2

◇ Illustrative assumption The bridge's fixed loss (3 W), switching-loss fraction (1.5 % of apparent power) and resistance (0.08 Ω) are illustrative numbers. All inverter losses are also supplied through the DC-DC stage, which has its own losses.

Single-phase, two-wire

The machine has two output leads, Lead A and Lead B. The AC set-point is the RMS voltage between those two leads. It is not a three-phase line-to-line voltage, and 415 V is just the top of the machine's range, not a statement about household supply.

What Volta Lab does with it

  • With the AC/DC switch on AC the chain is battery → DC-DC stage → DC link → H-bridge → output; on DC the bridge is bypassed.
  • The default output frequency is 50 Hz (an assumed default; the equations support others).
  • The output current limit is 10 A RMS, with a protective trip described in Model limitations and safety.

What it does not do

The model assumes an ideal, perfectly filtered sine wave: no harmonic distortion, no dead-time effects, no switching waveforms. There is no grid connection, no synchronisation to a grid, no anti-islanding behaviour, and no three-phase output.
