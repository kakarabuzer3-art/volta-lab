Model Limitations and Safety

Volta Lab is a teaching tool. It runs entirely in your browser and cannot generate, measure or deliver real electricity. Please read this page before you trust any number it shows.

Simulation only. This page explains a software model. It is educational material, not engineering or safety advice.

Simulation only

Every voltage, current, power and temperature on screen is computed by a software model. Nothing is measured from hardware, and the simulator cannot power an LED or any real device. Do not use it to make decisions about real wiring, protection or equipment design.

The cable-contact test is a fault

Touching the two cable ends together while the power is on simulates a terminal short circuit. That is a fault condition, not how a lamp or appliance is connected. To model a normal load you attach a load across the leads instead.

  • ◇ Illustrative assumption The contact between the leads has a resistance of 0.05 Ω.
  • ◇ Illustrative assumption The converter limits the output current to 10 A. If it stays at that limit for 2 seconds, the machine trips and latches off.
  • A latched trip is reset by switching main power off and on again.
  • The spark you see is a visual effect only; it is not a calculation of a real arc.

Other protections modelled

The battery model has a low-voltage cutoff, an empty state, an over-discharge current limit and an over-temperature limit. The converter has thermal shutdown with a restart temperature below the shutdown temperature. Attached loads move through normal, warning, overloaded, overheating, protection and damaged states, judged only against ratings the load actually specifies.

What is not simulated

  • Solar panels, maximum-power-point tracking or any solar charger.
  • Mains supply, grid connection or grid synchronisation.
  • Three-phase systems.
  • Battery charging, ageing or thermal runaway.
  • Individual switching waveforms, harmonics or electromagnetic interference.
  • Real electric arcs or the effects of electric shock on a person.

Real-world safety

Mains-level voltage can kill. Real arc and shock hazards depend on the source's impedance, the contact geometry, insulation, the environment and the exposure, none of which this model represents. Never copy wire-touching or sparking experiments with a real supply. Work on real electrical equipment only if you are qualified and follow the applicable standards and local regulations.
