Help

Volta Lab is an educational simulator of a battery, a DC-DC converter and a single-phase inverter. Everything you see is computed by a software model; no real electricity is involved.

How to operate the machine

  1. Main Power. Turn the switch ON (Space, the rocker on the machine, or the Main Power switch in Controls). The output stage is disabled until you do.
  2. AC or DC. Pick the output type with the AC / DC buttons, the lever on the machine or the M key. AC is single-phase, two-wire, 50 Hz, shown as RMS volts between the two leads. DC is a steady voltage.
  3. Voltage. Set 0 to 415 V with the slider, the preset buttons, the Up and Down arrow keys (5 V steps) or by dragging the front dial.

Connecting and touching the wires

Click or tap one highlighted cable tip, then the other, to bring them together. You can also drag a tip, or use the Touch wire ends and Separate buttons (keys T and S). With power ON, touching the ends simulates a terminal short circuit: the model limits the current and then trips. Turn Main Power off, then on, to reset a trip.

Simulation only. A short circuit is a fault, not how you connect a lamp or appliance. Never copy wire-touching or sparking experiments with a real supply.

Limitations & Safety

Electrical model assumptions

  • A simulated lithium-ion pack (equivalent-circuit model) feeds a DC-DC stage and, in AC mode, a single-phase H-bridge inverter.
  • AC is single-phase, two-wire, 50 Hz, with the RMS value measured between Lead A and Lead B. It is not a three-phase line-to-line voltage.
  • The output is limited to 10 A and trips after a sustained short. The trip stays latched until Main Power is turned off and on.
  • There is no charging, no solar input, no mains supply and no grid connection.

Limitations

  • Limits, resistances and the battery pack are model assumptions, not measured data.
  • The spark is visual only. This is not a model of real fault currents, arc energy or safe touch distances.
  • Touching the two cable ends together is a simulated terminal short circuit, not a normal lamp or appliance connection.
